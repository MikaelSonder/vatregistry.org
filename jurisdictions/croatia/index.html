VAT in Croatia, locally known as PDV (porez na dodanu vrijednost), is the structured transactional tax function through which taxable supplies of goods and services are classified, invoiced, reported and documented within the Croatian and EU VAT framework. It is broader than return filing alone because businesses must determine whether Croatian VAT registration is required, whether the domestic small-business exemption applies, whether Croatian VAT should appear on invoices, how input VAT deduction applies and how fiscalization evidence must be maintained.

Operationally, Croatian VAT usually begins with business model analysis rather than return preparation. A business reviews domestic supplies, imports, exports, intra-Community acquisitions, intra-Community supplies, B2B or B2C services, tourism and hospitality activity, e-commerce, digital transactions and mixed taxable or exempt supplies, then aligns registration, invoices, ePorezna processes, Fiscalization 2.0 reporting, accounting logic and filing obligations with the actual commercial flow.

The Croatian VAT framework combines the VAT Act, tax procedure rules, Porezna uprava guidance, ePorezna administration, the Fiscalization 2.0 regime and EU VAT law. Croatian VAT compliance is shaped by rate selection, place-of-supply analysis, reverse charge treatment, customer-status verification, structured e-invoices, real-time fiscalization and coordinated EU reporting.

Cross-border relevance is substantial. Croatia is an EU VAT territory with strong tourism, Adriatic maritime, logistics and regional trade relevance, where intra-Community trade, imports, exports, customer qualification, invoice evidence, fiscalization and filing discipline interact within one compliance architecture.

Definition

VAT in Croatia is the structured indirect tax function through which taxable business transactions are assessed, charged, documented and reported under Croatian and EU VAT rules. It concerns the tax treatment of supplies, purchases and goods movements rather than business profit, and it affects domestic commerce, international trade, invoicing processes and transaction evidence.

The practical importance of the VAT function lies in its recurring operational nature. It is not limited to one registration event or one annual tax exercise, but instead runs continuously through sales flows, procurement processes, bookkeeping codes, invoice issuance, reporting periods and cross-border transaction control.

Country Characteristics

Country characteristics explain the jurisdiction-specific features that shape how VAT operates in Croatia. This matters because Croatian VAT compliance depends not only on legislation, but also on administrative practice, EU integration and the practical expectations placed on business records and reporting discipline.

Croatia is an EU-integrated Adriatic jurisdiction with strong tourism, hospitality, maritime, logistics and regional trade relevance. VAT treatment operates through rate analysis, ePorezna administration, structured eRačun invoices, Fiscalization 2.0 reporting, logistics records and coordinated EU compliance.

Croatia applies a standard VAT rate of 25 percent and reduced rates of 13 percent and 5 percent for defined categories of goods and services. A domestic small-business exemption is generally available where annual turnover does not exceed EUR 60,000; non-established businesses do not generally benefit from that domestic threshold where Croatian VAT registration is required. From 1 January 2026, VAT-registered businesses established in Croatia must issue and receive structured e-invoices for domestic B2B transactions and fiscalize the relevant data under the Fiscalization 2.0 regime. B2C fiscalization has also expanded to invoices regardless of payment method.

Operational CultureCroatian VAT compliance is documentation-based, digitally administered and closely connected to structured eRačun invoices, ePorezna filing, real-time fiscalization and input VAT evidence.
Legal Framework OrientationThe system combines the Croatian VAT Act, tax procedure rules, Porezna uprava interpretation, Fiscalization Act rules, ePorezna processes and EU VAT law.
Commercial ContextTourism, domestic B2B transactions, intra-Community trade, imports, exports, e-commerce and EU business integration can make Croatian VAT analysis more complex than purely domestic sales treatment.
Language ExpectationCroatian is central to domestic administrative practice, ePorezna and Fiscalization 2.0 processes, while English is common in tourism, international advisory work and group-level tax coordination.

FAQ

The FAQ section collects recurring threshold questions in concise handbook form.

Is VAT in Croatia only relevant for Croatian companies?No. Foreign companies may also need Croatian VAT analysis where they trade into Croatia or create Croatian VAT consequences.
Is VAT the same as corporate income tax?No. VAT is a transactional indirect tax, while corporate income tax concerns business profit.
Does every invoice automatically require Croatian VAT?No. The correct treatment depends on the transaction, the parties, the place of supply and any exemption or reverse charge rule that may apply.
Is return filing enough on its own?No. Effective Croatian VAT compliance also depends on structured invoices, Fiscalization 2.0 reporting where applicable, records, coding logic, customer-status analysis and supporting documentation.
Can cross-border trade make Croatian VAT more complex?Yes. EU trade, OSS, imports, exports, e-commerce, tourism-related transactions and Fiscalization 2.0 data requirements can create additional analysis and reporting layers.
What are the principal VAT rates in Croatia?The standard rate is 25 percent. Reduced rates of 13 percent and 5 percent apply to specified categories under Croatian VAT law.
Does Croatia have a small-business VAT exemption?Yes. A domestic small-business exemption generally applies where annual turnover does not exceed EUR 60,000. Foreign businesses should separately assess Croatian registration and OSS obligations.
What is Fiscalization 2.0?Fiscalization 2.0 is Croatia's digital e-invoicing and transaction-reporting regime. From 1 January 2026, VAT-registered businesses established in Croatia must issue and receive structured e-invoices for domestic B2B transactions and meet related fiscalization obligations.
