VAT in Greece, locally known as FPA (ΦΠΑ), is the structured transactional tax function through which taxable supplies of goods and services are classified, invoiced, reported and documented within the Greek and EU VAT framework. It is broader than return filing alone because businesses must determine whether Greek VAT registration is required, whether Greek VAT should appear on invoices, how input VAT recovery applies, how myDATA reporting affects records and how transaction evidence must be maintained.

Operationally, Greek VAT usually begins with business model analysis rather than return preparation. A business reviews whether it is making domestic supplies, imports, exports, intra-Community acquisitions, intra-Community supplies, B2B or B2C services, digital transactions, island-connected supplies or mixed taxable and exempt supplies, then aligns registration, invoice configuration, myDATA transmission, accounting logic and reporting obligations with the actual commercial flow.

The Greek VAT framework combines the Greek VAT Code, tax procedure rules, AADE guidance, the myDATA electronic-books environment and EU VAT law. Greek VAT compliance is shaped by place-of-supply analysis, rate selection, reverse charge treatment, customer-status verification, invoice reporting and coordinated EU reporting requirements.

Cross-border relevance is substantial. Greece is an EU VAT territory and an important maritime, tourism and regional trade jurisdiction, where intra-Community trade, imports, exports, customer qualification, logistics, invoice reporting, input VAT recovery and filing discipline interact within one compliance architecture.

Definition

VAT in Greece is the structured indirect tax function through which taxable business transactions are assessed, charged, documented and reported under Greek and EU VAT rules. It concerns the tax treatment of supplies, purchases and goods movements rather than business profit, and it affects domestic commerce, international trade, invoicing processes and transaction evidence.

The practical importance of the VAT function lies in its recurring operational nature. It is not limited to one registration event or one annual tax exercise, but instead runs continuously through sales flows, procurement processes, bookkeeping codes, invoice issuance, reporting periods and cross-border transaction control.

Country Characteristics

Country characteristics explain the jurisdiction-specific features that shape how VAT operates in Greece. This matters because Greek VAT compliance depends not only on legislation, but also on administrative practice, EU integration and the practical expectations placed on business records and reporting discipline.

Greece is an EU-integrated Mediterranean economy with strong tourism, maritime, services and cross-border trade relevance. VAT treatment operates not only through local invoicing and returns, but also through rate analysis, myDATA digital books, logistics records, customer qualification and coordinated EU compliance.

Greece applies a standard VAT rate of 24 percent and reduced rates of 13 percent and 6 percent for defined categories of supplies. A small-enterprise VAT exemption is available subject to a EUR 10,000 annual turnover threshold; an exempt business generally does not charge VAT or recover input VAT. The myDATA electronic-books platform is a core operational feature of Greek VAT compliance, and mandatory B2B e-invoicing is being phased in during 2026.

Operational CultureGreek VAT compliance is documentation-based, digitally administered and closely connected to tax invoices, myDATA electronic-books reporting, return accuracy and input VAT evidence.
Legal Framework OrientationThe system combines the Greek VAT Code, tax procedure rules, AADE interpretation, myDATA processes and the EU VAT Directive framework.
Commercial ContextTourism, maritime activity, island supplies, imports, exports, OSS, digital activity and EU business integration can make Greek VAT analysis more complex than purely domestic sales treatment.
Language ExpectationGreek is central to domestic tax administration and myDATA operations, while English is common in international advisory work, tourism-related business and group-level tax coordination.

FAQ

The FAQ section collects recurring threshold questions in concise handbook form.

Is VAT in Greece only relevant for Greek companies?No. Foreign companies may also need Greek VAT analysis where they trade into Greece or create Greek VAT consequences.
Is VAT the same as corporate income tax?No. VAT is a transactional indirect tax, while corporate income tax concerns business profit.
Does every invoice automatically require Greek VAT?No. The correct treatment depends on the transaction, the parties, the place of supply and any exemption or reverse charge rule that may apply.
Is return filing enough on its own?No. Effective Greek VAT compliance also depends on invoices, myDATA reporting where applicable, records, coding logic, customer-status analysis and supporting documentation.
Can cross-border trade make Greek VAT more complex?Yes. EU trade, OSS, imports, exports, digital activity, customer-status verification and myDATA reporting can create additional analysis and reporting layers.
What are the principal VAT rates in Greece?The standard rate is 24 percent. Reduced rates of 13 percent and 6 percent apply to specified goods and services, subject to the conditions in Greek VAT law.
Does Greece have a small-business VAT exemption?Yes. A small-enterprise exemption is available subject to a EUR 10,000 annual turnover threshold. An exempt business generally does not charge VAT and cannot recover input VAT.
What is myDATA?myDATA is AADE's electronic-books platform, used to transmit and reconcile business documents and accounting data within the Greek tax-administration environment.
