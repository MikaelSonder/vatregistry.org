GST in Alberta is the federal transaction-tax function through which taxable supplies, imports and business purchases are classified, invoiced, reported and documented under Canada’s GST system. Alberta has no provincial sales tax or HST component. GST work is broader than return filing alone because businesses must determine whether registration is required, whether 5 percent GST applies, how input tax credits apply and how transaction evidence must be maintained.

Operationally, GST in Alberta usually begins with business model analysis rather than with tax form preparation. A business commonly reviews whether it is making domestic sales, imports, exports, intra-EU acquisitions, intra-EU dispatches, B2B services, consumer-facing supplies, digital transactions or mixed supplies, and then aligns registration, invoicing, accounting logic and reporting obligations with the actual commercial flow.

Alberta GST operates under the federal Excise Tax Act and is administered by the Canada Revenue Agency. It applies a single 5 percent federal GST, with no Alberta PST and no HST. Compliance is shaped by place-of-supply rules, taxable, zero-rated and exempt classification, small-supplier registration, input tax credits, tax-invoice requirements, self-assessment, imported services and interprovincial transactions.

Cross-border relevance is substantial. Alberta is a major Canadian energy, agriculture, technology, services and logistics jurisdiction. Alberta GST must be coordinated with federal GST registration, CBSA import treatment, place-of-supply rules, zero-rated exports, remote services, customer location and separate sales-tax systems in other Canadian provinces.

Definition

GST in Alberta is the federal indirect-tax function through which taxable supplies, imports and business purchases are assessed, charged, documented and reported under Canadian GST rules. It concerns transaction taxes rather than business profit and affects Alberta commerce, interprovincial trade, imports, exports, invoices and transaction evidence.

The practical importance of GST lies in its recurring operational nature. It runs through sales, purchases, place-of-supply rules, tax invoices, input tax-credit controls, reporting periods, imports, exports and interprovincial transaction control.

FAQ

The FAQ section collects recurring threshold questions in concise handbook form.

Is GST in Alberta only relevant for Alberta companies?No. Foreign companies may also need Alberta GST analysis where they trade into Alberta or create Alberta GST consequences.
Is GST the same as income tax?No. GST is a transactional consumption tax, while federal and Alberta income taxes concern income or profit under separate rules.
Does every invoice automatically require Alberta GST?No. The correct treatment depends on GST registration, supply classification, place of supply, zero-rating or exemption status, customer location and federal GST rules.
Is return filing enough on its own?No. Effective GST compliance also depends on invoices, records, coding logic, customer status analysis and supporting documentation.
Can interprovincial and international trade make Alberta GST more complex?Yes. Goods deliveries, service location, customer address, imports, exports, remote services and multi-province activity can create additional registration, rate, input-tax-credit and reporting layers.
What is the GST rate in Alberta?Alberta applies only 5 percent federal GST. It has no provincial sales tax and no HST component, making 5 percent the general consumption-tax rate for taxable supplies made in Alberta.
When is GST registration required?Most businesses must register when worldwide taxable supplies exceed CAD 30,000 in a single calendar quarter or over four consecutive calendar quarters. Registration is voluntary below the small-supplier threshold.
Does Alberta have a provincial sales tax?No. Alberta has no PST, retail sales tax or HST component. The federal 5 percent GST is the only general sales tax on taxable supplies in Alberta.
What are input tax credits?Input tax credits allow a GST registrant to recover eligible GST paid or payable on business purchases and expenses, subject to documentary, business-use and statutory requirements.
