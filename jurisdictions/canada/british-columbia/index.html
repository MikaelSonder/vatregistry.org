GST/PST in British Columbia is the combined federal and provincial transaction-tax function through which taxable supplies, imports and business purchases are classified, invoiced, reported and documented under Canada’s GST framework and British Columbia’s PST system. It is broader than return filing alone because businesses must separately determine whether GST registration and PST registration are required, whether 5 percent GST and 7 percent PST apply, whether a purchase is for resale or business use and how transaction evidence must be maintained.

Operationally, GST/PST in British Columbia usually begins with business model analysis rather than with tax form preparation. A business commonly reviews whether it is making domestic sales, imports, exports, intra-EU acquisitions, intra-EU dispatches, B2B services, consumer-facing supplies, digital transactions or mixed supplies, and then aligns registration, invoicing, accounting logic and reporting obligations with the actual commercial flow.

British Columbia’s system combines the federal Excise Tax Act for GST, the Provincial Sales Tax Act for PST, Ministry of Finance guidance, eTaxBC administration, CRA GST requirements and CBSA import procedures. Compliance is shaped by the 5 percent GST, 7 percent PST, separate registration thresholds, taxable goods, software and services, resale and business-use exemptions, customer location, marketplace rules and interprovincial transactions.

Cross-border relevance is substantial. British Columbia is a major Pacific trade, technology, resource, consumer, e-commerce and import jurisdiction. GST/PST must be coordinated with CRA registration, eTaxBC PST registration, CBSA import treatment, customer location, remote-seller rules, marketplace models, inventory, delivery evidence and other Canadian provincial systems.

Definition

GST/PST in British Columbia is the federal and provincial indirect-tax function through which taxable supplies, imports and business purchases are assessed, charged, documented and reported under Canadian GST and British Columbia PST rules. It concerns transaction taxes rather than business profit and affects British Columbia commerce, interprovincial trade, imports, exports, invoices and transaction evidence.

The practical importance of GST/PST lies in its recurring operational nature. It runs through sales, purchases, goods, software, taxable services, tax invoices, resale documentation, GST input tax credits, PST exemptions, reporting periods, imports and interprovincial transaction control.

FAQ

The FAQ section collects recurring threshold questions in concise handbook form.

Is GST/PST in British Columbia only relevant for British Columbia companies?No. Foreign companies may also need British Columbia GST/PST analysis where they trade into British Columbia or create British Columbia GST/PST consequences.
Is GST/PST the same as income tax?No. GST/PST is a transaction-tax system, while federal and provincial income taxes concern income or profit under separate rules.
Does every invoice automatically require British Columbia GST/PST?No. The correct treatment depends on separate GST/PST registration, goods/software/service classification, customer location, resale or exemption status, marketplace role and other applicable rules.
Is return filing enough on its own?No. Effective GST/PST compliance also depends on invoices, records, coding logic, customer status analysis and supporting documentation.
Can interprovincial and international trade make British Columbia GST/PST more complex?Yes. Customer location, out-of-province sellers, imports, Pacific trade, software, online marketplaces, e-commerce and multi-province activity can create separate GST, PST, customs and reporting layers.
What are the GST and PST rates in British Columbia?GST is 5 percent and PST is 7 percent. The combined rate is commonly 12 percent, although PST treatment depends on the particular goods, software, services or exemption.
Why is PST different from GST?GST is a federal value-added tax with input tax credits for registrants. PST is a separate provincial retail sales tax and is generally a cost to businesses buying taxable items for their own use; it is not generally recovered through input tax credits.
When is BC PST registration required for an out-of-province seller?An out-of-province or non-resident seller generally must register when specified sales into BC exceed CAD 10,000 in the preceding 12 months or are expected to exceed CAD 10,000 in the next 12 months. Inventory in BC can create registration duties regardless of the threshold.
Does the federal CAD 30,000 GST threshold apply to BC PST?No. The CAD 30,000 GST small-supplier threshold is a federal GST rule. BC PST uses its own CAD 10,000 threshold and separate registration criteria.
Do online marketplace facilitators need to register for BC PST?Yes, a marketplace facilitator can be required to register and collect PST on facilitated sales when its BC revenue exceeds CAD 10,000. The Provincial Sales Tax Act includes specific marketplace registration provisions.
