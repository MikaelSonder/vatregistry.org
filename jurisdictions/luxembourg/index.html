VAT in Luxembourg, locally known as TVA, is the structured transactional tax function through which taxable supplies of goods and services are classified, invoiced, reported and documented within the Luxembourg and EU VAT framework. It is broader than return filing alone because businesses must determine whether Luxembourg VAT registration is required, whether the small-business franchise applies, which rate applies, whether Luxembourg VAT should appear on invoices, how input VAT deduction applies and how transaction evidence must be maintained.

Operationally, Luxembourg VAT usually begins with business model analysis rather than return preparation. A business reviews domestic supplies, imports, exports, intra-Community acquisitions, intra-Community supplies, B2B or B2C services, financial services, digital transactions, fund-related activity or mixed taxable and exempt supplies, then aligns registration, invoices, accounting logic, eTVA/eCDF filing and reporting obligations with the actual commercial flow.

The Luxembourg VAT framework combines the amended VAT Law of 12 February 1979, tax procedure rules, AED guidance, eTVA and eCDF electronic filing systems and EU VAT law. Luxembourg VAT compliance is shaped by rate selection, place-of-supply analysis, reverse charge treatment, customer-status verification, invoice controls, input VAT recovery and coordinated EU reporting.

Cross-border relevance is substantial. Luxembourg is an EU VAT territory and an international financial, fund-management and cross-border services hub, where intra-Community trade, imports, exports, reverse charge, customer qualification, invoice evidence, input VAT recovery and electronic filing discipline interact within one compliance architecture.

Definition

VAT in Luxembourg is the structured indirect tax function through which taxable business transactions are assessed, charged, documented and reported under Luxembourg and EU VAT rules. It concerns the tax treatment of supplies, purchases and goods movements rather than business profit, and it affects domestic commerce, international trade, invoicing processes and transaction evidence.

The practical importance of the VAT function lies in its recurring operational nature. It is not limited to one registration event or one annual tax exercise, but instead runs continuously through sales flows, procurement processes, bookkeeping codes, invoice issuance, reporting periods and cross-border transaction control.

Country Characteristics

Country characteristics explain the jurisdiction-specific features that shape how VAT operates in Luxembourg. This matters because Luxembourg VAT compliance depends not only on legislation, but also on administrative practice, EU integration and the practical expectations placed on business records and reporting discipline.

Luxembourg is an EU-integrated international financial and cross-border services hub with substantial fund, banking, insurance, e-commerce and regional logistics relevance. VAT treatment operates through rate analysis, AED administration, eTVA/eCDF filing, invoice controls, input VAT recovery, financial-services exemption analysis and coordinated EU compliance.

Luxembourg applies a standard VAT rate of 17 percent, the lowest standard rate in the European Union, together with an intermediate rate of 14 percent, a reduced rate of 8 percent and a super-reduced rate of 3 percent for specified supplies. The national small-business franchise applies up to EUR 50,000 of annual turnover excluding VAT, with a 10 percent tolerance up to EUR 55,000 in defined circumstances. VAT returns and recapitulative statements must be filed electronically through the AED’s eTVA and eCDF systems; B2G electronic invoicing is mandatory, while ordinary B2B and B2C e-invoicing is not generally mandatory.

Operational CultureLuxembourg VAT compliance is documentation-based, electronically administered and closely connected to eTVA/eCDF filing, invoice discipline, input VAT evidence and cross-border transaction control.
Legal Framework OrientationThe system combines the amended VAT Law of 12 February 1979, tax procedure rules, AED interpretation, eTVA/eCDF processes and EU VAT law.
Commercial ContextFinancial services, fund structures, cross-border services, imports, exports, e-commerce and EU business integration often make Luxembourg VAT analysis more complex than purely domestic sales treatment.
Language ExpectationFrench is central to much domestic VAT legislation and administration, while Luxembourgish, German and English are all relevant in Luxembourg’s multilingual commercial, financial and international advisory environment.

FAQ

The FAQ section collects recurring threshold questions in concise handbook form.

Is VAT in Luxembourg only relevant for Luxembourg companies?No. Foreign companies may also need Luxembourg VAT analysis where they trade into Luxembourg or create Luxembourg VAT consequences.
Is VAT the same as corporate income tax?No. VAT is a transactional indirect tax, while corporate income tax concerns business profit.
Does every invoice automatically require Luxembourg VAT?No. The correct treatment depends on the transaction, the parties, the place of supply and any exemption or reverse charge rule that may apply.
Is return filing enough on its own?No. Effective Luxembourg VAT compliance also depends on invoices, eTVA/eCDF filings, records, coding logic, customer-status analysis, exemption analysis and supporting documentation.
Can cross-border trade make Luxembourg VAT more complex?Yes. EU trade, reverse charge, financial-services structures, OSS, imports, exports and multi-country activity can create additional analysis and reporting layers.
What are the principal VAT rates in Luxembourg?The standard rate is 17 percent. Luxembourg also applies an intermediate rate of 14 percent, a reduced rate of 8 percent and a super-reduced rate of 3 percent to specified supplies.
Does Luxembourg have a small-business VAT franchise?Yes. The national small-business franchise applies up to EUR 50,000 of annual turnover excluding VAT, with a defined 10 percent tolerance up to EUR 55,000. Foreign businesses must separately assess Luxembourg registration and OSS obligations.
What are eTVA and eCDF?eTVA and eCDF are electronic systems used for Luxembourg VAT administration. VAT returns and recapitulative statements are filed electronically with the AED through these systems.
