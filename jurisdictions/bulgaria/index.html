VAT in Bulgaria, locally referred to as DDS (данък върху добавената стойност), is the structured transactional tax function through which taxable supplies of goods and services are classified, invoiced, reported and documented within the Bulgarian and EU VAT framework. It is broader than return filing alone because businesses must determine whether Bulgarian VAT registration is required, whether the domestic SME regime applies, whether Bulgarian VAT should appear on invoices, how input VAT deduction applies and how transaction evidence must be maintained.

Operationally, Bulgarian VAT usually begins with business model analysis rather than return preparation. A business reviews domestic supplies, imports, exports, intra-Community acquisitions, intra-Community supplies, B2B or B2C services, digital transactions, e-commerce, warehouse arrangements and mixed taxable or exempt supplies, then aligns registration, VAT invoices, accounting codes, VAT returns, VIES reporting and evidence with the actual commercial flow.

The Bulgarian VAT framework combines the Value Added Tax Act, tax procedure rules, National Revenue Agency guidance, electronic tax services and EU VAT law. Bulgarian VAT compliance is shaped by rate selection, place-of-supply analysis, reverse charge treatment, customer-status verification, invoice controls, VIES reporting and coordinated EU compliance.

Cross-border relevance is substantial. Bulgaria is an EU VAT territory at the intersection of EU, Balkan and Black Sea trade routes, where intra-Community trade, imports, exports, logistics, customer qualification, VAT identification, invoice evidence and filing discipline interact within one compliance architecture.

Definition

VAT in Bulgaria is the structured indirect tax function through which taxable business transactions are assessed, charged, documented and reported under Bulgarian and EU VAT rules. It concerns the tax treatment of supplies, purchases and goods movements rather than business profit, and it affects domestic commerce, international trade, invoicing processes and transaction evidence.

The practical importance of the VAT function lies in its recurring operational nature. It is not limited to one registration event or one annual tax exercise, but instead runs continuously through sales flows, procurement processes, bookkeeping codes, invoice issuance, reporting periods and cross-border transaction control.

Country Characteristics

Country characteristics explain the jurisdiction-specific features that shape how VAT operates in Bulgaria. This matters because Bulgarian VAT compliance depends not only on legislation, but also on administrative practice, EU integration and the practical expectations placed on business records and reporting discipline.

Bulgaria is an EU-integrated jurisdiction with regional logistics, IT-services, e-commerce, manufacturing and Black Sea trade relevance. VAT treatment operates through rate analysis, National Revenue Agency administration, VAT identification, electronic filing, VIES reporting, logistics evidence and coordinated EU compliance.

Bulgaria applies a standard VAT rate of 20 percent and a reduced rate of 9 percent for specified categories, including qualifying accommodation and certain publications and baby products. From 1 January 2026, the national VAT-registration threshold is legally stated as EUR 51,130, the euro equivalent of the former BGN 100,000 threshold; taxable turnover is measured from the start of the calendar year, and the registration application is generally due within seven days of exceeding the threshold. Particular registration triggers can also apply to intra-Community acquisitions and cross-border B2B services under Article 97a of the VAT Act.

Operational CultureBulgarian VAT compliance is documentation-based, rate-sensitive and closely connected to National Revenue Agency e-services, VAT returns, VIES reporting, invoice discipline and input VAT evidence.
Legal Framework OrientationThe system combines the Value Added Tax Act, tax procedure rules, National Revenue Agency interpretation, VAT return and VIES rules and EU VAT law.
Commercial ContextRegional logistics, intra-Community services, imports, exports, e-commerce, digital activity and EU business integration often make Bulgarian VAT analysis more complex than purely domestic sales treatment.
Language ExpectationBulgarian is central to domestic administrative practice and tax filings, while English is common in international advisory work, IT services and group-level tax coordination.

FAQ

The FAQ section collects recurring threshold questions in concise handbook form.

Is VAT in Bulgaria only relevant for Bulgarian companies?No. Foreign companies may also need Bulgarian VAT analysis where they trade into Bulgaria or create Bulgarian VAT consequences.
Is VAT the same as corporate income tax?No. VAT is a transactional indirect tax, while corporate income tax concerns business profit.
Does every invoice automatically require Bulgarian VAT?No. The correct treatment depends on the transaction, the parties, the place of supply and any exemption or reverse charge rule that may apply.
Is return filing enough on its own?No. Effective Bulgarian VAT compliance also depends on invoices, VIES reporting where applicable, records, coding logic, customer-status analysis and supporting documentation.
Can cross-border trade make Bulgarian VAT more complex?Yes. EU trade, Article 97a, VIES, imports, exports, e-commerce and multi-country activity can create additional analysis and reporting layers.
What are the principal VAT rates in Bulgaria?The standard rate is 20 percent. A reduced rate of 9 percent applies to specified categories under the Bulgarian VAT Act.
When is Bulgarian VAT registration required?From 1 January 2026, the national registration threshold is EUR 51,130 of taxable turnover accumulated in the calendar year. The application is generally due within seven days after the threshold is exceeded, while other registration triggers can apply regardless of that threshold.
What is Article 97a registration?Article 97a of the Bulgarian VAT Act can require a Bulgarian taxable person to register for VAT in defined cross-border situations, including receiving certain B2B services from abroad or making certain EU B2B services, even where the ordinary domestic threshold has not been reached.
