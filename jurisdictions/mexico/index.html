VAT in Mexico, locally known as IVA (Impuesto al Valor Agregado), is the structured transactional tax function through which taxable acts or activities, imports and business purchases are classified, invoiced, reported and documented under Mexican law. It is broader than return filing alone because businesses must determine their RFC registration, whether Mexican IVA should appear on a CFDI invoice, how creditable IVA applies and how electronic invoice evidence must be maintained.

Operationally, Mexican IVA usually begins with business model analysis rather than monthly-return preparation. A business reviews domestic sales, imports, exports, B2B or B2C services, digital transactions, marketplace activity, manufacturing, border-region activity and mixed taxable or exempt supplies, then aligns RFC registration, e.firma credentials, CFDI 4.0 invoicing, PAC certification, accounting logic, creditable IVA and reporting obligations with the actual commercial flow.

Mexico’s IVA framework combines the Ley del Impuesto al Valor Agregado, Federal Fiscal Code requirements, SAT guidance, the CFDI 4.0 electronic-invoice regime, PAC certification and customs-linked import rules. Mexican IVA compliance is shaped by the 16 percent general rate, zero-rating, exemptions, the border-region fiscal stimulus, creditable-IVA conditions, withholding, digital-services rules, invoice validation and monthly reporting.

Cross-border relevance is substantial. Mexico is a major North American manufacturing, nearshoring, trade, e-commerce and digital-services jurisdiction, where imports, exports, IMMEX operations, customs records, CFDI invoices, border-region stimulus conditions, creditable IVA and SAT filing discipline operate as one compliance architecture.

Definition

VAT in Mexico is the structured indirect tax function through which taxable business transactions are assessed, charged, documented and reported under Mexican IVA rules. It concerns the tax treatment of supplies, purchases and goods movements rather than business profit, and it affects domestic commerce, international trade, invoicing processes and transaction evidence.

The practical importance of the VAT function lies in its recurring operational nature. It is not limited to one registration event or one annual tax exercise, but instead runs continuously through sales flows, procurement processes, bookkeeping codes, invoice issuance, reporting periods and cross-border transaction control.

Country Characteristics

Country characteristics explain the jurisdiction-specific features that shape how VAT operates in Mexico. This matters because Mexican IVA compliance depends not only on legislation, but also on administrative practice, EU integration and the practical expectations placed on business records and reporting discipline.

Mexico is a major North American manufacturing, nearshoring, trade, consumer, e-commerce and export-oriented jurisdiction. IVA treatment operates through RFC registration, SAT administration, e.firma credentials, CFDI 4.0 invoices, PAC validation, creditable IVA, customs documentation and monthly tax compliance.

Mexico applies a general IVA rate of 16 percent. A 0 percent rate applies to qualifying exports and specified supplies including many foodstuffs, medicines, books and agricultural activities, while defined activities such as residential rent, medical services, education and certain financial services are exempt. The commonly described 8 percent border rate is an effective rate produced by a fiscal stimulus equal to 50 percent of the 16 percent IVA, available only for qualifying activities in the Northern or Southern Border Region and subject to SAT registration and conditions. Mexico has no general turnover threshold: taxable businesses must register with SAT, obtain an RFC and issue CFDI electronic invoices from the start of taxable activity.

Operational CultureMexican IVA compliance is CFDI-driven, digitally administered and closely connected to RFC and e.firma controls, PAC validation, monthly returns, creditable-IVA evidence and customs records.
Legal Framework OrientationThe system combines Mexican IVA legislation, tax procedure rules, authority interpretation and EU VAT structure.
Commercial ContextNorth American trade, IMMEX manufacturing, imports, exports, digital activity, e-commerce, border-region operations and international business integration often make Mexican IVA analysis more complex than purely domestic sales treatment.
Language ExpectationSpanish is central to Mexican tax administration, CFDI documents and SAT practice, while English is common in international advisory work, manufacturing groups and North American trade coordination.

FAQ

The FAQ section collects recurring threshold questions in concise handbook form.

Is IVA in Mexico only relevant for Mexican companies?No. Foreign businesses may also need Mexican IVA, CFDI, customs or digital-services analysis where their activity creates Mexican tax consequences.
Is IVA the same as corporate income tax?No. IVA is a transactional indirect tax, while corporate income tax concerns business profit.
Does every invoice automatically require Mexican IVA?No. The correct treatment depends on the transaction, the parties, the place of supply and any exemption or reverse charge rule that may apply.
Is return filing enough on its own?No. Effective Mexican IVA compliance also depends on RFC registration, e.firma, valid CFDI, PAC validation, records, coding logic, customs evidence and supporting documentation.
Can cross-border trade make Mexican IVA more complex?Yes. North American trade, IMMEX, imports, exports, digital services, CFDI, customs and multi-country activity can create additional analysis and reporting layers.
What is the general IVA rate in Mexico?The general IVA rate is 16 percent. Qualifying exports and certain goods and activities are zero-rated, while defined activities including residential rent, medical services, education and certain financial services are exempt.
Is the 8 percent border rate a separate statutory IVA rate?No. The 8 percent result is generally a fiscal stimulus equal to 50 percent of the 16 percent IVA, available for qualifying activities in defined Northern and Southern Border Regions subject to SAT registration and conditions.
Is there an IVA registration threshold in Mexico?No. Mexico has no general turnover threshold for taxable activity. Businesses must register with SAT, obtain an RFC and establish CFDI capability from the beginning of taxable operations.
What is CFDI?CFDI (Comprobante Fiscal Digital por Internet) is Mexico's mandatory electronic tax invoice. A valid CFDI 4.0 is generated electronically, validated and stamped by an authorised PAC, and contains a unique UUID and SAT-required tax data.
