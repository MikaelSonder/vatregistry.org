Sales Tax in Washington is the state and local transaction-tax function through which retail sales, taxable services, use-tax liabilities and Business & Occupation (B&O) tax obligations are classified, collected, reported and documented under Washington law. It is broader than return filing alone because businesses must determine whether Washington nexus exists, whether DOR registration is required, whether sales tax must be collected at the customer location, how reseller permits apply and how transaction evidence must be maintained.

Operationally, Sales Tax in Washington usually begins with business model analysis rather than with tax form preparation. A business commonly reviews whether it is making domestic sales, imports, exports, intra-EU acquisitions, intra-EU dispatches, B2B services, consumer-facing supplies, digital transactions or mixed supplies, and then aligns registration, invoicing, accounting logic and reporting obligations with the actual commercial flow.

Washington’s transaction-tax framework combines the Revised Code of Washington, DOR rules and guidance, retail sales and use tax, local tax, Business & Occupation Tax, nexus provisions, marketplace-facilitator law and reseller-permit rules. Compliance is shaped by product and service taxability, physical and economic nexus, destination sourcing, local rates, B&O classification, marketplace roles, registration, reseller permits, use tax and periodic returns.

Cross-border relevance is substantial. Washington is a major U.S. technology, e-commerce, trade, port, logistics and import jurisdiction. Washington retail sales tax, use tax and B&O tax must be coordinated with marketplace models, warehouse inventory, destination sourcing, reseller permits, customs arrangements and sales-tax obligations in other U.S. states.

Definition

Sales Tax in Washington is the state and local indirect-tax function through which retail sales, taxable services, digital products, purchases, use-tax liabilities and B&O tax obligations are assessed, collected, documented and reported under Washington law. It concerns transaction taxes rather than income tax and affects retail commerce, e-commerce, technology, inventory, interstate trade, imports and transaction evidence.

The practical importance of Washington’s transaction taxes lies in their recurring operational nature. They run through sales flows, product and service taxability, digital products, fulfilment, delivery location, marketplace activity, B&O classifications, reseller purchases, use-tax accruals, accounting codes, returns and multistate control.

FAQ

The FAQ section collects recurring threshold questions in concise handbook form.

Is Sales Tax in Washington only relevant for Washington companies?No. Foreign companies may also need Washington sales tax analysis where they trade into Washington or create Washington sales tax consequences.
Is Washington B&O tax the same as income tax?No. Washington B&O tax is a gross-receipts tax, not a net-income tax. It is separate from retail sales tax, use tax and federal income tax.
Does every sale automatically require Washington sales tax?No. The correct treatment depends on nexus, product or service taxability, reseller or exemption status, delivery location, marketplace role and other Washington rules.
Is return filing enough on its own?No. Effective sales-tax and B&O compliance also depends on invoices, records, coding logic, customer status analysis and supporting documentation.
Can multistate and international trade make Washington taxes more complex?Yes. Warehouses, ports, fulfilment, marketplace sales, imports, interstate deliveries, software, digital products, B&O tax and multistate activity can create additional nexus, taxability, use-tax and reporting layers.
What is Washington’s state retail sales-tax rate?The state rate is 6.5 percent. Cities and counties impose additional local tax, so the combined rate depends on where the customer receives the goods or services and should be verified against current DOR rate information.
When does Washington economic nexus arise?A remote seller or marketplace facilitator has economic nexus when gross receipts sourced or attributed to Washington exceed USD 100,000 in the current or prior calendar year. Washington has no transaction-count threshold.
What is Washington B&O tax?Business & Occupation Tax is a Washington gross-receipts tax imposed on business activity. It is separate from retail sales and use tax and can apply to remote sellers; eligible remote sellers without local physical activity may have a No Local Activity deduction.
What is a Washington reseller permit?A valid reseller permit allows a purchaser to buy qualifying goods or services for resale without paying retail sales tax. The purchaser provides the permit to the seller, who must retain it as supporting documentation.
Do marketplace facilitators collect Washington sales tax?Marketplace facilitators meeting Washington’s USD 100,000 nexus threshold generally collect and remit retail sales tax on facilitated taxable sales. Direct sales outside the marketplace remain relevant to a seller’s own nexus and B&O-tax analysis.
