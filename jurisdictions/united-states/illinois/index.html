Sales Tax in Illinois is the state and local transaction-tax function through which retail sales of tangible personal property, taxable purchases and use-tax liabilities are classified, collected, reported and documented under Illinois law. The primary retailer-level tax is Retailers’ Occupation Tax (ROT), not a VAT. The subject is broader than return filing alone because businesses must determine whether Illinois nexus exists, whether registration is required, whether destination-based state and local ROT must be collected, how resale certificates apply and how transaction evidence must be maintained.

Operationally, Sales Tax in Illinois usually begins with business model analysis rather than with tax form preparation. A business commonly reviews whether it is making domestic sales, imports, exports, intra-EU acquisitions, intra-EU dispatches, B2B services, consumer-facing supplies, digital transactions or mixed supplies, and then aligns registration, invoicing, accounting logic and reporting obligations with the actual commercial flow.

Illinois’s sales-and-use-tax framework combines the Retailers’ Occupation Tax Act, Use Tax Act, Illinois Department of Revenue rules and publications, local ROT rules, remote-seller requirements and marketplace-facilitator law. Compliance is shaped by product taxability, physical and economic nexus, destination sourcing, local tax, registration, Certificates of Resale, marketplace roles, use tax and periodic returns.

Cross-border relevance is substantial. Illinois is a major U.S. consumer, manufacturing, logistics, transportation, e-commerce and warehouse jurisdiction. Illinois ROT and use tax must be coordinated with delivery locations, marketplace models, inventory, local taxes, resale documentation, customs arrangements and sales-tax obligations in other U.S. states.

It concerns transaction taxes rather than business profit and affects retail commerce, e-commerce, inventory, interstate trade, imports and transaction evidence.

The practical importance of ROT and use tax lies in its recurring operational nature. It runs through sales flows, product taxability, fulfilment, delivery destinations, marketplace sales, resale purchases, use-tax accruals, accounting codes, returns and multistate transaction control.

Country Characteristics

Country characteristics explain the jurisdiction-specific features that shape how VAT operates in Illinois. This matters because Illinois sales tax compliance depends not only on legislation, but also on administrative practice, EU integration and the practical expectations placed on business records and reporting discipline.

Illinois is a major U.S. consumer, manufacturing, logistics, transport, e-commerce and warehouse jurisdiction. Its sales-and-use-tax system combines state and local Retailers’ Occupation Tax rather than a federal VAT. Compliance depends on nexus, product taxability, customer delivery location, destination sourcing, local rates, marketplace rules, inventory location, exemption documentation and IDOR administration.

Illinois applies a 6.25 percent state Retailers’ Occupation Tax rate, made up of 5 percent state tax and 1.25 percent local tax. Additional local ROT can apply, so destination-based sales may carry a higher combined rate. From 1 January 2026, a remote retailer or marketplace facilitator has economic nexus solely when cumulative gross receipts from sales of tangible personal property to Illinois purchasers reach USD 100,000 or more in the preceding 12-month period. The former 200-transaction test has been removed. The test is assessed quarterly at the end of March, June, September and December. Remote retailers collect and remit state and local ROT at the destination rate; failure to maintain adequate location data or schedules for destination-based sales can result in taxation at a 15 percent rate.

FAQ

The FAQ section collects recurring threshold questions in concise handbook form.

Is Sales Tax in Illinois only relevant for Illinois companies?No. Foreign companies may also need Illinois sales tax analysis where they trade into Illinois or create Illinois sales tax consequences.
Is Illinois sales tax the same as income tax?No. Illinois Retailers’ Occupation Tax and Use Tax are transaction taxes, while Illinois income tax and federal income tax are separate tax regimes.
Does every sale automatically require Illinois ROT?No. The correct treatment depends on nexus, product taxability, resale or exemption status, delivery location, marketplace role and other Illinois rules.
Is return filing enough on its own?No. Effective Sales-tax compliance also depends on invoices, records, coding logic, customer status analysis and supporting documentation.
Can multistate and international trade make Illinois sales tax more complex?Yes. Warehouses, fulfilment, marketplace sales, imports, interstate deliveries, destination sourcing and multistate operations can create additional nexus, rate, use-tax and reporting layers.
What is Illinois Retailers’ Occupation Tax?Retailers’ Occupation Tax, often referred to as Illinois sales tax, is imposed on the business of selling tangible personal property at retail. Retailers collect the tax from customers and remit it to IDOR; purchasers may owe Use Tax when tax was not properly collected.
What is the Illinois state sales-tax rate?Illinois has a 6.25 percent state ROT rate, comprising 5 percent state tax and 1.25 percent local tax. Additional local ROT may apply to destination-based sales, so the combined rate can be higher.
When does Illinois economic nexus arise?From 1 January 2026, a remote retailer has economic nexus when cumulative gross receipts from sales of tangible personal property to Illinois purchasers are USD 100,000 or more in the preceding 12 months. The former 200-transaction test no longer applies. The test is assessed quarterly.
Why is destination information important?Remote retailers collect state and local ROT at the destination rate. From 2026, taxpayers unable to provide supporting location information or schedules for destination-based sales can face taxation at a 15 percent rate on affected receipts.
What is Form CRT-61?Form CRT-61 is Illinois’s Certificate of Resale. A purchaser provides it to support a tax-free resale purchase, and the seller must retain it as evidence; IDOR can request it during review.
