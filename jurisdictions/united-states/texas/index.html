Sales Tax in Texas is the state and local transaction-tax function through which retail sales, leases and rentals of taxable goods and taxable services, together with use-tax liabilities, are classified, collected, reported and documented under Texas law. It is broader than return filing alone because businesses must determine whether Texas nexus exists, whether a sales-and-use-tax permit is required, whether the correct state and local tax must be collected, how resale certificates apply and how transaction evidence must be maintained.

Operationally, Sales Tax in Texas usually begins with business model analysis rather than with tax form preparation. A business commonly reviews whether it is making domestic sales, imports, exports, intra-EU acquisitions, intra-EU dispatches, B2B services, consumer-facing supplies, digital transactions or mixed supplies, and then aligns registration, invoicing, accounting logic and reporting obligations with the actual commercial flow.

Texas’s sales-and-use-tax framework combines the Texas Tax Code, Comptroller rules and guidance, local taxing-jurisdiction rules and U.S. nexus principles. Compliance is shaped by product and service taxability, physical and economic nexus, origin and destination sourcing, local-tax caps, marketplace-provider treatment, permits, resale certificates, exemption documentation, use tax and periodic returns.

Cross-border relevance is substantial. Texas is a major U.S. consumer, energy, technology, manufacturing, logistics and international-trade jurisdiction. Texas sales and use tax must be coordinated with marketplace models, warehouse inventory, local sourcing, resale documentation, import arrangements and sales-tax obligations in other U.S. states.

Definition

Sales Tax in Texas is the state and local indirect-tax function through which retail sales, taxable services, leases, rentals, taxable purchases and use-tax liabilities are assessed, collected, documented and reported under Texas law. It concerns transaction taxes rather than business profit and affects retail commerce, e-commerce, energy and equipment transactions, inventory, interstate trade, imports and transaction evidence.

The practical importance of sales and use tax lies in its recurring operational nature. It runs through sales flows, taxable-service classification, equipment and lease transactions, fulfilment, shipping location, marketplace activity, resale purchases, use-tax accruals, accounting codes, returns and multistate transaction control.

Country Characteristics

Country characteristics explain the jurisdiction-specific features that shape how VAT operates in Texas. This matters because Texas sales tax compliance depends not only on legislation, but also on administrative practice, EU integration and the practical expectations placed on business records and reporting discipline.

Texas is a major U.S. consumer, energy, technology, manufacturing, logistics and international-trade jurisdiction. Its sales-and-use-tax system is state and local rather than federal, and compliance depends on nexus, product and service taxability, origin and destination sourcing, local-tax limits, marketplace rules, inventory location, resale documentation and Comptroller administration.

Texas imposes a 6.25 percent state sales-and-use-tax rate on taxable retail sales, leases and rentals of most goods and taxable services. Cities, counties, special-purpose districts and transit authorities can impose local tax up to a combined maximum of 2 percent, producing a maximum combined rate of 8.25 percent. A remote seller must obtain a Texas permit and collect tax if its total Texas revenue is USD 500,000 or more during the preceding 12 calendar months; the threshold is revenue-only, with no transaction-count test, and marketplace sales count toward the calculation. Texas has distinctive sourcing rules: a seller with a Texas place of business generally uses origin sourcing for local tax, while remote sellers generally use destination sourcing and may elect the 1.75 percent single local use-tax rate if eligible.

FAQ

The FAQ section collects recurring threshold questions in concise handbook form.

Is Sales Tax in Texas only relevant for Texas companies?No. Foreign companies may also need Texas sales tax analysis where they trade into Texas or create Texas sales tax consequences.
Is sales tax the same as franchise or income tax?No. Texas sales and use tax is a transaction tax, while Texas franchise tax and federal income tax are separate tax regimes.
Does every sale automatically require Texas sales tax?No. The correct treatment depends on nexus, product or service taxability, resale or exemption status, sourcing, marketplace role and other Texas rules.
Is return filing enough on its own?No. Effective Sales-tax compliance also depends on invoices, records, coding logic, customer status analysis and supporting documentation.
Can multistate and international trade make Texas sales tax more complex?Yes. Warehouses, fulfilment, marketplace sales, imports, interstate deliveries, taxable services and multistate activity can create additional nexus, sourcing, use-tax and reporting layers.
What is Texas’s state sales-tax rate?Texas imposes a 6.25 percent state sales-and-use-tax rate. Local taxing jurisdictions may impose up to 2 percent combined local tax, producing a maximum combined rate of 8.25 percent.
When does Texas economic nexus arise?A remote seller must obtain a permit and collect Texas tax when total Texas revenue is USD 500,000 or more in the preceding 12 calendar months. The test is revenue-only, with no transaction-count threshold; marketplace sales count toward the calculation.
Can a Texas remote seller use one local tax rate?An eligible remote seller may elect the single local use-tax rate, currently 1.75 percent, rather than determining the local rate for each Texas destination. Marketplace providers are not eligible for this election.
What is a Texas resale certificate?A properly completed Form 01-339, Texas Sales and Use Tax Resale Certificate, can support tax-free resale purchases. A seller’s responsibility is not satisfied merely by receiving a customer’s permit number or a copy of a permit.
