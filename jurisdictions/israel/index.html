VAT in Israel, locally known as Ma’am (מע״מ), is the structured transactional tax function through which taxable transactions, imports and business purchases are classified, invoiced, reported and documented under Israeli VAT law. It is broader than return filing alone because businesses must determine the appropriate dealer status, whether Israeli VAT should appear on invoices, how input VAT deduction applies, whether an Israel Invoices allocation number is required and how transaction evidence must be maintained.

Operationally, Israeli VAT usually begins with business model analysis rather than return preparation. A business reviews domestic supplies, imports, exports, B2B or B2C services, digital transactions, e-commerce, professional activity and mixed taxable or exempt supplies, then aligns dealer status, VAT registration, tax invoices, SHAAM allocation numbers, accounting logic, input VAT deduction and reporting obligations with the actual commercial flow.

Israel’s VAT framework combines the Value Added Tax Law, 5736-1975, tax procedure requirements, Israel Tax Authority guidance, the Israel Invoices allocation-number regime and customs-linked import rules. Israeli VAT compliance is shaped by the 18 percent rate, exempt-dealer versus authorized-dealer status, invoice controls, input VAT deduction, zero-rated exports, non-resident registration and periodic returns.

Cross-border relevance is substantial. Israel is a Middle Eastern technology, services, trade and innovation jurisdiction, where import VAT, zero-rated exports, non-resident representation, SHAAM invoice controls, customs records, input VAT deduction and Tax Authority filing discipline operate as one compliance architecture.

Definition

VAT in Israel is the structured indirect tax function through which taxable business transactions are assessed, charged, documented and reported under Israeli VAT rules. It concerns the tax treatment of supplies, purchases and goods movements rather than business profit, and it affects domestic commerce, international trade, invoicing processes and transaction evidence.

The practical importance of the VAT function lies in its recurring operational nature. It is not limited to one registration event or one annual tax exercise, but instead runs continuously through sales flows, procurement processes, bookkeeping codes, invoice issuance, reporting periods and cross-border transaction control.

Country Characteristics

Country characteristics explain the jurisdiction-specific features that shape how VAT operates in Israel. This matters because Israeli VAT compliance depends not only on legislation, but also on administrative practice, EU integration and the practical expectations placed on business records and reporting discipline.

Israel is a technology, services, innovation, consumer and international-trade jurisdiction with significant cross-border business activity. VAT treatment operates through dealer classification, Israel Tax Authority administration, tax invoices, SHAAM allocation numbers, input VAT deduction, customs records and export or non-resident supply analysis.

Israel applies a standard VAT rate of 18 percent. There are no reduced positive VAT rates; supplies are generally standard-rated, zero-rated or exempt. Every dealer must register before commencing business, but a qualifying small dealer may operate as an exempt dealer (osek patur) where annual turnover does not exceed ILS 122,833 in 2026; the exempt dealer does not charge VAT or deduct input VAT. A dealer above the ceiling, or in a profession excluded from exempt-dealer status, operates as an authorized dealer (osek murshe). From 1 June 2026, qualifying B2B tax invoices above ILS 5,000 require a SHAAM allocation number under the Israel Invoices regime.

FAQ

The FAQ section collects recurring threshold questions in concise handbook form.

Is VAT in Israel only relevant for Israeli businesses?No. Foreign businesses may also need Israeli VAT, customs and fiscal-representation analysis where they make supplies connected with Israel.
Is VAT the same as corporate income tax?No. VAT is a transactional indirect tax, while corporate income tax concerns business profit.
Does every invoice automatically require Israeli VAT?No. The correct treatment depends on the transaction, the parties, the place of supply and any exemption or reverse charge rule that may apply.
Is return filing enough on its own?No. Effective Israeli VAT compliance also depends on dealer status, tax invoices, SHAAM allocation numbers where required, records, coding logic, input VAT evidence and supporting documentation.
Can cross-border trade make Israeli VAT more complex?Yes. Imports, exports, technology and digital services, non-resident activity, fiscal representation, SHAAM invoice rules and multi-country transactions can create additional analysis and reporting layers.
What is the standard VAT rate in Israel?The standard VAT rate is 18 percent. There are no reduced positive rates; supplies are generally standard-rated, zero-rated or exempt depending on their legal classification.
What is an exempt dealer (osek patur)?An exempt dealer is a qualifying small business that does not charge VAT or deduct input VAT. The indexed annual turnover ceiling is ILS 122,833 for 2026, subject to excluded professions and other statutory conditions.
What is an authorized dealer (osek murshe)?An authorized dealer charges VAT on taxable supplies, files periodic VAT returns and can generally deduct eligible input VAT. A dealer above the exempt-dealer ceiling, or excluded from exempt status, operates under this regime.
What is the Israel Invoices allocation number?Israel Invoices is a VAT-control model that requires an advance SHAAM allocation number for qualifying B2B tax invoices. From 1 June 2026, the threshold is above ILS 5,000 before VAT, subject to the applicable rules.
