VAT in South Korea, locally known as bugagachise (부가가치세), is the structured transactional tax function through which taxable supplies, imports and business purchases are classified, invoiced, reported and documented under Korean VAT law. It is broader than return filing alone because businesses must determine business-registration status, whether the general or simplified taxpayer regime applies, whether Korean VAT should appear on invoices, how input tax credits apply and how electronic tax-invoice evidence must be maintained.

Operationally, Korean VAT usually begins with business model analysis rather than return preparation. A business reviews domestic supplies, imports, exports, B2B or B2C services, digital transactions, e-commerce, platform activity and mixed taxable or exempt supplies, then aligns business registration, general or simplified taxpayer status, e-tax invoices, Hometax procedures, accounting logic and reporting obligations with the actual commercial flow.

The Korean VAT framework combines the Value-Added Tax Act, tax procedure rules, National Tax Service guidance, Hometax administration and electronic tax-invoice rules. Korean VAT compliance is shaped by general versus simplified taxation, supply classification, 10 percent rate application, tax-invoice requirements, input tax-credit conditions, cross-border digital-services treatment, customs records and return filing.

Cross-border relevance is substantial. South Korea is a major Asia-Pacific manufacturing, technology, import and export jurisdiction, where import VAT, zero-rated exports, e-tax invoices, input tax credits, customs records and National Tax Service filing discipline operate as one compliance architecture.

Definition

VAT in South Korea is the structured indirect tax function through which taxable business transactions are assessed, charged, documented and reported under Korean VAT rules. It concerns the tax treatment of supplies, purchases and goods movements rather than business profit, and it affects domestic commerce, international trade, invoicing processes and transaction evidence.

The practical importance of the VAT function lies in its recurring operational nature. It is not limited to one registration event or one annual tax exercise, but instead runs continuously through sales flows, procurement processes, bookkeeping codes, invoice issuance, reporting periods and cross-border transaction control.

Country Characteristics

Country characteristics explain the jurisdiction-specific features that shape how VAT operates in South Korea. This matters because Korean VAT compliance depends not only on legislation, but also on administrative practice, EU integration and the practical expectations placed on business records and reporting discipline.

South Korea is a major manufacturing, technology, consumer and export-oriented Asia-Pacific jurisdiction. VAT treatment operates through business-registration status, general or simplified taxpayer classification, electronic tax invoices, Hometax administration, input tax-credit controls, customs records and National Tax Service procedures.

South Korea applies a standard VAT rate of 10 percent, with zero-rated treatment for qualifying exports and selected international transactions. There is no general turnover threshold for business registration: a business operator must apply for registration within 20 days of commencing business. Individual businesses with annual revenue below KRW 104 million may generally fall within the simplified taxpayer regime, subject to exclusions and sector conditions; simplified taxpayers use industry value-added ratios and have restricted input-credit and tax-invoice treatment. Electronic tax invoices are mandatory for corporations and for individual businesses above the applicable statutory threshold.

Operational CultureKorean VAT compliance is digital, documentation-based and closely connected to Hometax, electronic tax invoices, general or simplified taxpayer status, input tax-credit evidence and return discipline.
Legal Framework OrientationThe system combines the Value-Added Tax Act, tax procedure rules, National Tax Service interpretation, Hometax procedures, e-tax invoice rules and customs-linked requirements.
Commercial ContextImports, exports, cross-border digital activity, e-commerce, technology supply chains and international business activity often make Korean VAT analysis more complex than purely domestic sales treatment.
Language ExpectationKorean is central to domestic administration, Hometax and invoice practice, while English is common in international advisory work and group-level tax coordination.

FAQ

The FAQ section collects recurring threshold questions in concise handbook form.

Is VAT in South Korea only relevant for Korean companies?No. Foreign companies may also need Korean VAT analysis where they trade into South Korea or create Korean VAT consequences.
Is VAT the same as corporate income tax?No. VAT is a transactional indirect tax, while corporate income tax concerns business profit.
Does every invoice automatically require Korean VAT?No. The correct treatment depends on the transaction, the parties, the place of supply and any exemption or reverse charge rule that may apply.
Is return filing enough on its own?No. Effective Korean VAT compliance also depends on business registration, electronic tax invoices, Hometax processes, records, coding logic, input tax-credit evidence and supporting documentation.
Can cross-border trade make Korean VAT more complex?Yes. Imports, exports, e-commerce, digital services, customs records and multi-country activity can create additional analysis and reporting layers.
What is the standard VAT rate in South Korea?The standard VAT rate is 10 percent. Qualifying exports and certain international transactions may receive zero-rated treatment, subject to statutory conditions.
Is there a general VAT registration threshold?No. Korean law requires a business operator to apply for business registration within 20 days of commencing business. The KRW 104 million figure concerns eligibility for the simplified taxpayer regime, not an exemption from business registration.
What is the simplified taxpayer regime?Individual businesses below KRW 104 million of annual revenue may generally be eligible for simplified taxation, subject to exclusions and sector conditions. It uses industry value-added ratios and has restricted input-credit and tax-invoice treatment.
What is an electronic tax invoice?An electronic tax invoice (전자세금계산서) is a digital tax document issued through Hometax or an approved provider. It is mandatory for corporations and for individual businesses above the applicable statutory threshold.
